 **Comment**: P. 34: Def) Efficiency wage Theory
> Efficiency wage theories argue that productivity often increases with real wages; as a result, it does not pay firms to cut wages. High wages may raise productivity either because they attract higher quality labor; or because they result in increased effort; or because they reduce labor turnover and save on hiring and training costs. Efficiency wage theories can be used to explain why firms do not lower wages even in the presence of an excess supply of workers, and also why they avoid two-tier wage systems, under which new workers are hired at lower wages than existing workers.
